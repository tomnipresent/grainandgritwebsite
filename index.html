The science

Grain changes with the light.

Film grain comes from light hitting the silver halide crystals in the emulsion. The light varies across the frame, so the grain does too. An overlay lays the same texture across the whole frame, but real grain isn't uniform.

That's why grain peaks in the midtones, thins out in the shadows, and flattens in the highlights. Every film stock has its own response curve, its own distribution of where grain shows up and how much.

Grain & Grit Machine models this. Each stock carries its own measured response curve, and grain is rendered per-pixel from that data. Grain doesn't go on top of the image. It comes from the image.

FAQ

Questions.

How does the NLE integration work?+
GGM installs as a plugin inside Premiere, AE, and Resolve. Select clips, send to GGM, apply grain, rendered clips return to your project. No round-tripping.
Plugin or standalone?+
Both. The plugin connects to your NLE. The standalone app is where you preview, dial in, and render.
Is it a subscription?+
No. Perpetual licence. Pay once. All v1.x updates included.
Works on graded footage?+
Yes. GGM applies grain in linear light. Your grade is preserved.
GPU requirements?+
wgpu (Metal on macOS, Vulkan on Windows/Linux). Modern discrete GPU recommended for 4K+ preview.
How is this different from FilmConvert or Dehancer?+
Most tools use scanned grain plates or noise generators. GGM renders grain procedurally — per-pixel, from a physically modelled emulsion. The grain responds to your image.
Same grain every render?+
Yes. Deterministic, seeded, pixel-identical. Every time.
